## 2. Dominant modern crypto setups (SFP, deviations, sweeps, SMC/ICT, reclaims, breakout-retest, pullbacks)

### Takeaway
The shared core of 2020s crypto TA is **liquidity-based mean reversion at range extremes**: the swing failure pattern (SFP) and range deviation-and-reclaim, framed in SMC/ICT language as a "liquidity sweep." Entry triggers on a candle close back inside the level, and the stop goes beyond the sweep wick. Higher-timeframe (HTF) levels (weekly or daily) define the trade; lower timeframes (LTF) are used for entry.

### Cited Findings
- **Deviation and reclaim**: price trades beyond a range high or low, collects liquidity, then must "quickly reclaim the broken level and close back inside the range." Failure to reclaim invalidates the setup. — [Medium "Trading in the Range"](https://cryptoposeidonnn.medium.com/trading-in-the-range-3e52e4a8ba71); DonAlt's applied example: [Daily Hodl](https://dailyhodl.com/2023/04/23/trader-who-recently-unloaded-crypto-positions-warns-repeat-of-2021-bitcoin-meltdown-heres-his-downside-target/amp/)
- **SFP**: a wick through a prior swing high or low that closes back on the other side. Educational sources call it useful but not always reliable and recommend confluence. — [CoinGecko](https://www.coingecko.com/learn/swing-failure-pattern-trading); [CoinMarketCap Academy](https://coinmarketcap.com/academy/article/what-is-the-swing-failure-pattern-and-how-to-use-it-in-trading); [DEXTools](https://www.dextools.io/tutorials/swing-failure-pattern-sfp-how-to-trade-failed-breakouts)
- **Weekly SFP on BTC** as a HTF reversal signal (Mayne's count above). — [Webull/Benzinga](https://www.webull.ca/zh-hant/news-detail/14151323543749632)
- **SMC/ICT** (order blocks, FVGs, BOS/CHoCH): the most supported claim is that order blocks or FVGs work only with confluence, meaning a preceding sweep or displacement. Standalone OBs and FVGs show little edge (see Q6). — [Botsfolio SMC guide](https://botsfolio.com/learn/smart-money-concepts); [Backtrex OB+FVG confluence](https://backtrex.com/en/blog/order-block-fair-value-gap-smc-confluence); [Chartwhisperer](https://chartwhisperer.ca/blog/order-blocks-fair-value-gaps-smart-money-guide)
- **Liquidation-cluster sweeps**: a sweep is more likely when a liquidation cluster coincides with a key support or resistance level. — [OneBullEx academy](https://www.onebullex.com/academy/clusters-de-liquidacao-btc); [DEXTools liquidation maps 2026](https://www.dextools.io/tutorials/how-to-read-liquidation-maps-in-crypto-guide-2026)
- On-chain or low-liquidity tokens: Eugene says traditional TA "often fails" there, so these setups are a CEX/major-perp tool. — [TechFlow](https://www.techflowpost.com/en-US/newsletter/62657)

### Inferences
- In practice, SFP, deviation, and liquidity sweep are the same event under different names. Each offers a natural tight stop (beyond the wick) against a target at the opposite side of the range, which is why they pair naturally with "short stop, long profit."
- Breakout-retest and trend pullback are the continuation counterparts. I found no trader-specific primary documentation for them in this pass.

### Gaps
- No primary trader documentation of exact entry timeframes (for example, "HTF level + 15m close"). This is widely repeated folk practice but not sourced here.
- Breakout-retest and HTF-reclaim specifics from named traders were not found.

## 3. How order flow and derivatives data are used (OI, funding, liquidation heatmaps, CVD, spot vs perp premium, orderbook)

### Takeaway
Derivatives data is used as **context and confirmation, not a standalone signal**. The main uses: rising OI plus extreme funding marks crowded positioning to fade; spot-led CVD is read as healthier than perp-led moves; liquidation clusters mark sweep targets and places *not* to put stops. CryptoCred's key point is that price divergence from these metrics is what matters.

### Cited Findings
- CryptoCred: OI, liquidations, funding, and CVD all matter mainly through "the presence or lack of a price-based divergence." — [Medium guide](https://medium.com/@cryptocreddy/comprehensive-guide-to-crypto-futures-indicators-f88d7da0c1b5)
- CVD = market buys minus market sells, used to infer aggressive market-order participation. — [CryptoCred X](https://x.com/CryptoCred/status/1727996775930139028)
- **Spot vs perp CVD**: "when spot leads, the move is usually sustainable; when perps lead, usually fragile," and "when the two CVDs disagree, believe spot." A caveat: for BTC/ETH, sophisticated execution can muddy the picture. **[practitioner heuristic, no backtest]** — [WatchCrypto](https://watchcrypto.io/learn/cvd-cumulative-volume-delta); [Chartwhisperer CVD](https://chartwhisperer.ca/blog/cumulative-volume-delta-cvd-crypto-trading-guide)
- **Funding**: only extremes are actionable. Extreme positive funding reads as euphoria or crowded longs; extreme negative reads as capitulation. Healthy ranges "are not actionable signals." — [Chartwhisperer funding](https://chartwhisperer.ca/blog/funding-rates-perpetuals-trading-guide); [Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/learn/articles/what-bitcoin-sfunding-rate-really-tells-you)
- **OI**: every dollar of OI needs posted margin, so it is harder to fake than volume. Rising OI means new positions; falling OI with extreme funding means a weakening trend. — [Buildix glossary](https://www.buildix.trade/blog/crypto-orderflow-glossary-40-terms); [FXStreet](https://www.fxstreet.com/cryptocurrencies/news/why-is-everyone-in-the-crypto-market-talking-about-open-interest-and-funding-rates-202508070945)
- **Spot premium** (e.g., Coinbase premium) is a possible signal, but should not be used alone as proof of spot demand. — [Backquant derivatives guide](https://www.backquant.com/articles/derivatives-guide-1)
- **Liquidation heatmaps**: Coinglass is the free default. Hyblock (paid) estimates levels from the real-time OI distribution and filters by leverage. The two often disagree. Clusters "act like magnets." A heatmap "shows where forced orders would fire if price arrives, but does not tell you whether price will arrive." — [DEXTools](https://www.dextools.io/tutorials/how-to-read-liquidation-maps-in-crypto-guide-2026); [BloFin academy](https://blofin.com/academy/education/liquidation-heatmaps-order-flow); [Hyblock academy: Liquidation Levels](https://academy.hyblockcapital.com/tools/liquidation-levels)
- Hyblock's stop guidance: place stops just *beyond* visible liquidity, so the stop isn't hunted and doubles as invalidation. **[vendor education]** — [Hyblock academy](https://academy.hyblockcapital.com/tools/liquidation-levels)
- Exchange education on reading these metrics together. — [Binance Square: Understanding Derivatives Data](https://www.binance.com/en/square/post/274181); [TradingView crypto derivatives indicators](https://www.tradingview.com/blog/en/crypto-derivatives-indicators-on-tradingview-53558)

### Inferences
- The typical workflow combines an HTF level, a sweep or deviation of that level into a liquidation cluster with an OI flush or extreme funding, and an LTF reclaim. Stops then go beyond the sweep wick, outside the visible liquidity pool.

### Gaps
- No named-trader primary source (e.g., Skew's own posts) was retrieved. Skew is widely associated with orderbook and CVD reading, but that is unsourced here.
- No independent backtest of spot vs perp CVD divergence was found.

## 4. Stop placement and sizing (invalidation stops, fixed % risk, leverage, scaling, breakeven, drawdown, liquidation avoidance)

### Takeaway
Credible sources converge on four rules. (1) Place the stop at structural invalidation, beyond the wick or range, not at an arbitrary %. (2) Derive position size from the stop distance and the account risk %. (3) Use leverage as a sizing tool, not a bet multiplier, keeping the liquidation price well beyond the stop. (4) Accept frequent small stop-outs in exchange for large R-multiple winners.

### Cited Findings
- Invalidation-based stop plus a size formula, size = equity × risk% ÷ stop distance (worked example: 2% risk, 5% stop). — [CryptoCred via Scribd](https://www.scribd.com/document/734451687/Updated-Risk-Management)
- Risk per trade should vary with setup quality rather than stay rigidly fixed (CryptoCred). — [Scribd](https://www.scribd.com/document/734451687/Updated-Risk-Management)
- General guides cite 1–3% risk per trade and place stops "where the price proves the trade idea wrong." — [Crypto Briefing](https://cryptobriefing.com/betting-bitcoin-dont-forget-risk-management); [CryptoRank stop-loss guide](https://cryptorank.io/news/feed/4339f-stop-loss-crypto)
- "Strict stop-loss levels ... significantly higher profit targets ... profit multiples far exceeding losses ... more frequent stop-outs" (Eugene). — [TechFlow](https://www.techflowpost.com/en-US/newsletter/62657)
- Never move or ignore a stop. The SOL $210/$200 example. — [MEXC/PANews](https://www.mexc.com/en-GB/news/trader-eugenes-interview-transcript-newcomers-should-focus-on-on-chain-assets-first-as-most-of-the-market-does-not-care-about/230)
- Avoid ruin first: "minimizing the risk of bankruptcy" drives sizing (Eugene). Altcoin risk under 1% and leverage used "close to never" (Cobie). — [MEXC](https://www.mexc.com/news/230); [Bitget/Cobie](https://www.bitget.com/news/detail/12560605089004)
- Give stops "room for error" past the entry area (Daan). — [@DaanCrypto](https://x.com/DaanCrypto)
- Scaling out: some guides suggest spreading exits over 2–3 price zones, and ATR-based rather than fixed-% stops. **[generic guides]** — [Changelly](https://changelly.com/blog/risk-management-in-crypto-trading/); [Coinfomania](https://coinfomania.com/risk-management-tools-in-crypto-the-only-ones-that-actually-protect-your-capital/)
- Leaderboard evidence on leverage: many Hyperliquid top traders were wiped out, and leverage "can turn small adverse moves into total account destruction." One analysis of about 10k wallets found only 16.5% profitable **[Medium post, method unverified]**. A commentary recommends a 2% max risk per trade. — [Bitget](https://www.bitget.com/news/detail/12560605177924); [Envy Protocol Medium](https://medium.com/@envyprotocol/i-analyzed-10-000-hyperliquid-traders-the-results-are-brutal-a29adcca8c2a); [Health-niche commentary](https://health-niche.com/hyperliquid-leaderboard-gaming-why-winning-competitions-often-signals-irrational-risk-taking/)
- Eugene's 2026 response to a string of stop-outs: reduce trading frequency, be more patient (an implicit drawdown rule). — [PANews](https://www.panewslab.com/en/articles/019d32df-fcb3-76fa-8aae-56cb3e60b114)

### Inferences
- With an invalidation stop and a fixed risk %, a high nominal leverage (e.g., 10–20x) can still mean small account risk, *provided* the liquidation price sits beyond the stop. Blow-ups come from sizing to leverage rather than to the stop distance. This follows from the sizing formula above; no single source states it in these words.

### Gaps
- No named trader's explicit breakeven-stop rule or max-drawdown rule (e.g., "stop trading after −X% month") was found in primary sources.

## 6. Independent evidence: do these setups work?

### Takeaway
There is **no peer-reviewed evidence** that SFP, deviation, or SMC/ICT setups have an edge in crypto perps. Vendor backtests are mixed to negative for standalone order blocks and FVGs; confluence claims are unverified. Academic work on perps covers funding mechanics, funding arbitrage (not risk-free, and often unprofitable after costs), and liquidation cascades, not discretionary pattern edges.

### Cited Findings
- StatOasis codified four ICT entries mechanically on daily bars. FVG beat the simple-rule control on only 2 of 4 tests; other ICT families fell short. — [StatOasis](https://statoasis.com/overfit/research/ict-backtest-what-survives)
- FXNX tested 1,000 trades: unconfirmed order-block bounces failed 56.9% of the time. — [FXNX](https://fxnx.com/en/blog/smart-money-concepts-work-backtest-evidence)
- AlgoStorm: revisited order blocks held 82% of the time vs 78% for control zones (ES, 40 sessions) and 47% vs 51% (EUR/USD), so little edge over ordinary levels. — [AlgoStorm](https://algostorm.com/ict-smc-key-concepts/)
- Botsfolio: BTC 4H order blocks, 137 instances, 45% win rate, expectancy −0.15R. Its claimed 85–97% win rate for 1H OBs after a sweep is **[unverified, vendor]**. — [Botsfolio](https://botsfolio.com/learn/smart-money-concepts)
- Chartwhisperer: an FVG on its own "has not been shown to beat chance." Its own 78.6% / 10,899-trade figure comes from a proprietary protocol **[vendor]**. — [Chartwhisperer](https://chartwhisperer.ca/blog/order-blocks-fair-value-gaps-smart-money-guide)
- SFP: no formal study found. Mayne's 20/22 weekly SFP count is self-reported and small. — [Webull/Benzinga](https://www.webull.ca/zh-hant/news-detail/14151323543749632)
- Academic: He & Manela, "Fundamentals of Perpetual Futures," finds funding-rate arbitrage "is not risk-free even disregarding margin requirements and trading costs." — [arXiv 2212.06888](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.06888)
- MDPI Mathematics (2026), "Two-Tiered Structure of Cryptocurrency Funding Rate Markets": only 40% of top funding opportunities had positive returns after costs and spread reversals. — [MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2227-7390/14/2/346)
- arXiv preprint on seven BTCUSDT liquidation cascades (2022–25): early-warning signals differ from event to event, with OI and long/short ratios used as proxies. Binance's per-event liquidation snapshots are no longer served. — [arXiv 2607.27070](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.27070)

### Inferences
- The defensible part of the "new-generation" playbook is risk management: invalidation stops, stop-derived sizing, low effective leverage, and asymmetric R. The setup edge itself is unproven. Plausibly, the asymmetric stop/target structure matters more than the specific pattern, but no study isolates this.
